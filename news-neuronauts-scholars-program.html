NeuroNauts Scholars: how a summer neuroscience program for high school students works

Program updateNovember 24, 2026 by Rodney Guttmann

The NeuroNauts Scholars Program is an eight week summer research experience at the University of West Florida for high school juniors and seniors from Escambia and Santa Rosa Counties. It is funded by a five year R25 grant from the National Institute of Neurological Disorders and Stroke, part of the National Institutes of Health.

What scholars do

Each scholar works 40 hours per week in an active UWF research laboratory, paired with a faculty mentor based on their interests. The work is real, ongoing research, not a simulated exercise. Alongside lab time, scholars take part in weekly seminars, scientific writing and data interpretation workshops, and NIH required training on research ethics and responsible conduct. The summer ends with a formal research symposium where scholars present their work. Scholars receive a $4,500 stipend.

The first cohort

The inaugural program ran from June 1 through July 24, 2026. Eight scholars were chosen from more than 20 applicants, who came from nearly every high school in the Pensacola region.

How selection works

Selection starts with a faculty eligibility screen. A review panel then reads academic records, personal statements and teacher recommendations, and about a dozen finalists are interviewed in person on campus. The final group is chosen to reflect the region and to include students who will benefit most from the experience.

Learn more or apply

Applications open each spring. The program website has current details, deadlines and information about the 2026 cohort: NeuroNauts program website. The NeuroNauts program is directed by Dr. Rodney Guttmann of the UWF Department of Biology. You can also see how it fits into the lab's work on the Guttmann Research Lab home page.
